\begin{ChineseAbstract}
建筑群配置光伏、储能电池和生活热水储罐并共用供电设施时，日常调度需要同时兼顾住户电费、社区最高用电功率和室内温度舒适。在实际使用中，刻意压低整个社区的总电费开支，有时会在某一特定时段推高社区最高用电功率，也可能让个别楼栋出现过冷或过热的异常状况。对不同建筑群的控制效果进行横向比较时，天气与电价数据、算法参数、模型权重文件和运行日志往往分散存储在不同位置。依靠人工逐一翻查，很难查明指标差异具体出自哪一栋楼、发生在哪一个时段。本研究基于CityLearn开发了建筑群能源仿真评估平台。该平台可以分别存储社区共用的天气与分时段电价信息，单独保存每栋楼的用电功率和设备参数，同时支持历史数据表格导入以及日常运行指标上报。发布数据集时，后台会先对齐每栋楼的时间步并统一物理单位，再针对每栋楼单独训练下一时间步的室温预测模型。日常开展仿真实验时，研究人员只需在网页端提交配置参数，Spring Boot后端服务便会启动Python环境完成计算，MySQL数据库同步记录所用参数和任务运行状态。无控制基准、分层规则和多智能体强化学习都走同一套流程，每次运行生成的参数快照、模型权重文件和结果文件按任务编号归集在一起，方便事后复查。分级柔性约束能源控制（TFEC）沿用了社区分层能源系统协调算法（CHESCA）的分层思路，根据社区刚需用电功率相对历史均值的突增倍率，对制冷设备出力进行连续调节。针对仿真环境中生活热水储罐的状态更新错误，本研究单独开展了修复与有效性检验。区域协同多智能体强化学习（District-MARL）在单楼基础观测之外，补充了社区协同特征和削峰奖励，演员-注意力-评论家多智能体强化学习（AAC-MADRL）等控制器也一并接入平台作为对照。比较页面按楼列出电费支出和冷热不适比例，使用独立无控制基准计算综合评分，只允许所有楼栋室温均达标的任务进入排名。某项指标出现异常时，研究人员可直接调出对应楼栋的净用电曲线和设备动作记录，快速定位问题时段。在CityLearn本地与在线数据集的测试中，不开启储罐修复且其余削峰参数一致时，TFEC连续调节和单阈值削峰在本地测得的社区最高用电功率相同。在在线工况下，TFEC的最高用电功率、电费支出和碳排放都略低。开启储罐修复后，虽然电费与碳排放小幅上升，但两种规则的社区最高用电功率均继续下降，在线工况下TFEC相对单阈值削峰的最高用电功率差值进一步扩大。强化学习对照测试中，IPPO与MAPPO的社区最高用电功率和电费均低于无控制基准。District-MARL加入尖峰时段电池充放电计价规则后，大多数随机种子的峰值比下降，但当前样本规模下的配对检验尚未达到统计显著水平。用电高峰前的电池曲线还显示，District-MARL预留的剩余电量偏少。在线历史任务复核中，动作生效的时间步顺序、室温连续预测偏差和结束时的剩余储能量都会影响跨控制器比较。在线任务复核还将网页指标与保存的结果文件进行了核对。

\begin{EnglishAbstract}
When a \mbox{building-cluster} system equipped with photovoltaics, batteries, and domestic hot-water storage shares a power distribution feeder, daily operation must consider household electricity bills, community peak power demand, and indoor temperature comfort together. In practical use, lowering total community electricity bills can still push up community peak power demand in certain periods or cause individual buildings to become too cold or too warm. When comparing control performance across buildings, weather and electricity price data, algorithm parameters, as well as model weights and run logs are stored in separate locations, making it difficult to trace manually which building and period caused a metric difference. The CityLearn-based \mbox{building-cluster} energy simulation evaluation platform developed in this study can separately store community-shared weather and \mbox{time-of-use} electricity prices alongside per-building power demand and equipment parameters, supporting historical table import and daily indicator upload. Upon dataset publication, the backend aligns timestamps across buildings, unifies physical units, and trains a next-step indoor temperature predictor for each building. During use, researchers submit tasks through web pages, the Spring Boot backend schedules Python to execute control algorithms, and MySQL records tasks and configurations. The platform integrates no-control baselines, hierarchical rule-based methods, and multi-agent reinforcement learning controllers, saving parameter snapshots, model weights, and evaluation results under matching task identifiers. Tiered Flexible-Constraint Energy Control (TFEC) extends the Community-based Hierarchical Energy Systems Coordination Algorithm (CHESCA) by continuously scaling cooling actions according to the surge ratio of community non-shiftable power demand relative to its historical average. A state-update error in the domestic hot-water storage of the simulation environment is repaired and tested separately. District-level multi-agent reinforcement learning (District-MARL) adds community cooperative observations and peak-shaving rewards, while Actor-Attention-Critic Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (\mbox{AAC-MADRL}) and other controllers serve as baselines. The comparison page lists per-building bills and thermal discomfort proportions and computes a composite score referenced to an independent no-control baseline. Only tasks meeting indoor temperature comfort requirements in every building enter the ranking, and users can check net power curves and device actions to locate specific buildings and periods when anomalies appear. In factorial comparisons on local and online CityLearn benchmark datasets, when storage repair is disabled under identical peak-shaving parameters, TFEC continuous regulation matches single-threshold peak shaving in local community peak power demand and yields slightly lower community peak power demand, electricity bills, and carbon emissions online. Once storage repair is enabled, although electricity bills and carbon emissions rise slightly, community peak power demand drops further under both rules, and the online gap in peak power demand between TFEC and the single-threshold rule widens. In reinforcement learning comparisons, IPPO and MAPPO achieve lower community peak power demand and electricity bills than the no-control baseline. After adding peak-period battery pricing to District-MARL, peak ratios decline across most random seeds, though paired tests under the current sample size do not reach statistical significance. Pre-peak battery curves also show that District-MARL retains low remaining charge. In online historical task checks, action execution order, cumulative drift in indoor temperature prediction, and terminal remaining storage all affect cross-controller comparisons. During the online task review, the displayed metrics were also checked against the saved result files.
