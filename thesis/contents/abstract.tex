\begin{ChineseAbstract}
    多栋安装光伏、储能电池和生活热水储罐的建筑共用供电设施时，日常调度需要同时考虑住户电费、社区最高用电功率和室内温度舒适。若只关注压低社区总电费，多栋楼在低电价时段一起购电，容易推高某一时段的社区最高用电功率，过度限制购电又会让个别楼栋出现过冷或过热。比较不同控制算法时，天气电价数据、算法参数、模型权重和运行日志往往分散在不同目录，靠人工翻查很难查明指标差异出在哪栋楼、哪一时段。本研究基于CityLearn开发了建筑群能源仿真评估平台，将社区共用的天气与分时段电价和各楼用电功率、设备参数分开存储，支持历史表格导入和日常运行数据上报。数据齐备后可发布数据集，此时后台会先对齐各楼时间步并统一物理单位，然后再为每栋楼训练室温预测模型。当需要开启仿真任务时，研究人员会在网页设置并提交配置参数，启动任务后，后端会自动启动Python进程来执行对应的算法，并通过MySQL同步记录参数快照和任务状态。无控制基准、分层规则和多智能体强化学习使用同一套执行流程，每次运行的参数、模型权重和结果文件按任务进行存放并支持按任务统一查询，便于之后进行复查。控制策略方面，本文提出了分级柔性约束能源控制（TFEC），该算法沿用社区分层能源系统协调算法（CHESCA）的分层结构，按社区刚需用电功率相对历史均值的突增倍率连续调节制冷出力，并针对CityLearn中生活热水储罐的状态更新错误进行了单独修复与对比检验。平台同时还接入了区域协同多智能体强化学习（District-MARL）与演员-注意力-评论家多智能体强化学习（AAC-MADRL）等算法，其中District-MARL在单楼观测外补充了社区协同特征和削峰奖励。平台还配置了算法比较页面，页面中选择算法后，会按楼列出电费和冷热不适比例，并以独立无控制基准计算综合评分，指标异常时可直接调出对应楼栋的净用电曲线和设备动作定位问题时段。在CityLearn竞赛数据集测试中，未进行储罐状态更新修复时，TFEC的社区最高用电功率不高于单阈值削峰，电费与碳排放也略低。当开启修复后，两种规则的社区峰值继续下降，电费与碳排放小幅上升，TFEC的削峰优势还会进一步扩大。

    \ChineseKeywords{机器学习；建筑群能源管理；仿真评估；多智能体强化学习；实验管理；可视化分析}
\end{ChineseAbstract}

\begin{EnglishAbstract}
    When multiple buildings equipped with photovoltaics, batteries, and domestic hot-water storage share a power supply, daily operation must consider household electricity bills, community peak power demand, and indoor temperature comfort together. Focusing only on lowering total community electricity bills can cause multiple buildings to purchase power together during low-price periods and push up community peak power demand, while restricting power purchases too strictly may leave individual buildings too cold or too warm. When comparing control algorithms, weather and electricity price data, algorithm parameters, model weights, and run logs are often stored in separate directories, making it difficult to trace manually which building and period caused a metric difference. The CityLearn-based \mbox{building-cluster} energy simulation evaluation platform developed in this study separately stores community-shared weather and \mbox{time-of-use} electricity prices alongside per-building power demand and equipment parameters, supporting historical table import and daily operational data upload. Once the data are ready, a dataset can be published. The backend first aligns timestamps across buildings and unifies physical units, then trains an indoor temperature prediction model for each building. To run a simulation task, researchers configure and submit parameters through web pages. After the task starts, the backend launches a Python process to execute the selected algorithm, and MySQL records parameter snapshots and task status. No-control baselines, hierarchical rules, and multi-agent reinforcement learning share the same execution workflow, with parameters, model weights, and result files stored by task and queryable by task for later review. For control strategies, this study proposes Tiered Flexible-Constraint Energy Control (TFEC), which extends the hierarchical structure of the Community-based Hierarchical Energy Systems Coordination Algorithm (CHESCA) by continuously scaling cooling output according to the surge ratio of community non-shiftable power demand relative to its historical average. The state-update error of domestic hot-water storage in CityLearn is also repaired and tested separately. The platform further integrates District-level multi-agent reinforcement learning (District-MARL) and Actor-Attention-Critic Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (\mbox{AAC-MADRL}), where District-MARL adds community cooperative features and peak-shaving rewards to single-building observations. An algorithm comparison page is also provided. After selecting algorithms, the page lists per-building electricity bills and thermal discomfort proportions and computes a composite score referenced to an independent no-control baseline. When a metric appears abnormal, users can inspect the corresponding building's net power curve and device actions to locate the affected period. In tests on the CityLearn competition datasets, without storage repair, TFEC achieves a community peak power demand no higher than single-threshold peak shaving, with slightly lower electricity bills and carbon emissions. When storage repair is enabled, community peak power demand drops further under both rules, electricity bills and carbon emissions rise slightly, and TFEC's peak-shaving advantage expands further.

    \EnglishKeywords{machine learning; building-cluster energy management; simulation evaluation; multi-agent reinforcement learning; experiment management; visual analysis}
\end{EnglishAbstract}
